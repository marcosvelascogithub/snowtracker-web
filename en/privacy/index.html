SnowTracker

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 13, 2026

SnowTracker has no accounts and no servers of its own. Your sessions are stored on your device. The app has no ads and no tracking. It only sends technical crash reports and performance measurements, with no GPS location, health or personal data.

What data it collects and how

Location: the position, speed and altitude provided by your iPhone’s GPS, with your location permission. It’s collected while you record a session. In Today, the app reads a position only if you’ve already given permission; it never asks for it there.

Motion: the air pressure measured by your iPhone’s barometer, with your motion permission, while you record a session.

Health, on Apple Watch: with the Health permission you give on your iPhone, during the session, the watch app reads your heart rate, active calories and the distance of each run, and receives the skiing segments that Health detects. The watch sends them to your iPhone.

What you create in the app: your sessions and your app settings.

Crash reports: if the app crashes or hits an error, a technical report is sent: where the code failed, the iPhone model, the iOS and app versions, a randomly generated installation identifier, used to tell how many people a crash affects and whether it repeats, the app’s state at that moment (whether a session is running and for how long, the open tab, battery, language, units, and the status of permissions and Apple Watch) and a log of when the session started, paused, resumed or ended, in minutes from the start and without the time of day. It doesn’t include your position, speed, altitude, resort, heart rate, dates or your name.

Performance measurements (from version 3.0): how long the app takes to open, whether screens stutter, and how long some operations take on the iPhone, such as saving a session or finding runs to compare, with their duration only. They come with the iPhone model, the iOS and app versions, the language, the network type and carrier, processor and memory use, the same installation identifier and the IP address, which Firebase uses to tell the country. They don’t include your position, resort, pistes, heart rate, dates or your name.

The app doesn’t collect your name, your email, your contacts or any identifier to track you.

What it’s used for

Location: to measure speed, distance, altitude and vertical, to detect runs and lifts, and to draw and play back the route. In Today, to recognize the ski resort you're at, on your iPhone.

Motion: to measure vertical more precisely.

Health: to show your heart rate during the session, to show your heart rate and calories in the day’s summary, and to save them with the session. The Health segments, with the distance of each run, are saved with the session, but the app doesn’t show them or use them in the stats.

Crash reports: to find and fix the app’s bugs.

Performance measurements: to find and fix what is slow.

Notifications: only to let you know, on the iPhone itself during a session, that the battery is running low or, after 30 minutes without moving, to ask if you’re still skiing. They don’t send any data.

The data isn’t used for advertising, marketing or profiling.

Where it’s stored

The session history (summary, runs and route) is stored in the app’s private storage on your iPhone.

Heart rate, calories and the Health segments from the watch are stored separately, also on your iPhone, in a store excluded from all backups.

While you record, the app also keeps a session journal so it can recover the session if the app closes. The journal doesn’t include your heart rate, isn’t included in backups and is deleted once the session is saved.

If you have iPhone backup turned on, to iCloud or to a computer, the history is included in it, but the health data isn’t. If you restore your iPhone from a backup, you get your sessions back, but not their heart rate, calories or Health segments.

Who it’s shared with

The app doesn’t share your data with third parties: it has no servers of its own and includes no advertising services. The only other company’s services are crash reporting and performance measurements. Data only leaves your iPhone in these cases:

Health: only if you allow the app to save workouts to Health. Each session is saved in the Health app as a workout, with the route, the distance of the runs and, if available, heart rate and calories.

Apple Maps: to show maps, the app uses Apple Maps, which Apple handles according to its own privacy policy. Pistes, lifts and ski resorts (© OpenStreetMap contributors) are included in the app: showing them, recognizing the resort you're at and searching for another sends no data.

Share route: a session’s GPX file only leaves your iPhone when you share it yourself, and goes wherever you choose.

Crash reports: they are sent to Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, which processes them on the developer’s behalf and may do so in data centers in any country. See Firebase privacy.

Performance measurements: they are sent to Firebase Performance Monitoring, another Google service, on the same terms.

How long it’s kept and how to delete it

Sessions are kept until you delete them.

Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Performance Monitoring keep crash reports and performance measurements for the period Google states in Firebase privacy, which also explains how they are deleted.

To delete a session, swipe its row in History to the left and tap “Delete”, or open it and, at the end of the summary, tap “Delete Session”. To delete several, tap “Edit”, select the sessions and tap “Delete”. Its route, its runs and the watch data are deleted too and, if it was saved to Health, its workout.

Whatever is already in Health can also be deleted in the Health app.

How to withdraw consent

Location and notifications: tap their row in the app’s Settings, or go to iOS Settings. Motion: in iOS Settings › Privacy & Security.

Health: change the permissions in the Health app › Profile › Privacy › Apps › SnowTracker and, on the watch, in Settings › Health › Apps.

The app works without these permissions, though without the data that depends on them.
